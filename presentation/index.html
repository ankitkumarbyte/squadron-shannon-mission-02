Mission debrief

Three things we learned

🧩

Rules are safe because they are dumb

The FST is a perfect Lovelace machine. It never invented a value in 2,800 noisy sessions, and it never understood a joke either.

✨

LLMs construct, and they surprise

8/8 objective checks, including a sentence that works around its own constraint. That is origination of combinations, not yet of concepts.

📡

Meaning has redundancy that words lack

40 % of words but 78 % of meaning survived. The largest loss came from the sender's own compression.

Design we recommend: FST skeleton for control, LLM voice for language, noise decoder in between, and every value read back before it is locked.
Limits, said by us first: Gemini only (no paid Claude/GPT keys) · one run per probe · a lenient judge from the same family · gemini-3.8-flash overloaded on the day · word-substitution noise only.
In-class dialectic

Questions for discussion

Q1 · If an LLM passes every Anti-Turing probe, has it refuted Lovelace?

No, it has moved the question. Passing shows constraint-driven construction instead of retrieval, which refutes "it only repeats". Whether recombining learned rules counts as "originating" is a matter of definition. That is exactly where Lovelace and Turing disagreed.

Q2 · Why keep a brittle finite-state machine when LLMs understand language better?

Because its brittleness fails closed. In 2,800 noisy sessions it aborted many times and never locked a wrong course. An LLM fails open: it fills gaps fluently. So the FST owns every value, and the LLM only interprets and phrases.

Q3 · What does Shannon tell us about a 7-word conversation with one noise token?

At least 14 % of bandwidth is overhead (2.81 bits of entropy, zero meaning). Reliability needs redundancy where noise hits, but 7 words leave none, so we removed the predictable words and kept the high-surprisal ones. Then we added a checksum, the read-back confirmation.
